## Problem
A physics-informed neural network (PINN) learns airflow around an airfoil by enforcing the flow equations. Where the surface curves sharply the flow changes fast, so uniform sampling wastes training points.

## What I built
**Preprocessing** on the AirfRANS CFD dataset:
- Non-dimensionalised and z-scored the features, using training-set statistics only to avoid leakage.
- Computed airfoil curvature with a periodic smoothing spline and spread it into the flow field.
- Computed mesh gradients with a 12-nearest-neighbour weighted least-squares method.
- Labelled each node as surface, near-wall, wake or far-field, and compared three collocation samplers: uniform, mesh density and curvature-weighted.
- Split the data into train, validation and a blind set made of the thickest 15% of airfoils.

**Training and evaluation:**
- A PINN trained with a data loss, a no-slip boundary loss and a RANS physics residual loss, weighted by curvature.
- Hyperparameters tuned with Optuna.
- Evaluated on validation and unseen-shape airfoils, including pressure on the surface, lift and drag.

## Results
- 60 simulations processed with no NaN or infinite values, about 180,000 mesh nodes per simulation.
- Curvature and the pressure gradient near the wall correlated at about 0.72 (Spearman), which supports curvature-weighted sampling.
